MODULE 09 · COST OPTIMIZATION

Every second costs.
Know where it goes.

Module 1 introduced credits and warehouse sizes. This module goes much further: exactly how compute, storage and serverless features turn into a bill, the guardrails that stop runaway spend, the views that tell you who spent what, and a repeatable playbook for the most common interview scenario of all: "our Snowflake bill went up 40% last month. What do you do?"

Think of Snowflake as a serviced office building. Four characters explain the whole bill:

WAREHOUSE COMPUTE
The taxi meter

Runs while the car is moving and while it waits outside with the engine on. Bigger car = faster meter. Turn the engine off (suspend) and the meter stops.

STORAGE
The storage locker

A monthly rent per terabyte. Old copies you keep "just in case" (Time Travel, Fail-safe, clones) take up shelf space too.

SERVERLESS
The cleaning crew

Snowflake sends its own staff (Snowpipe, auto-clustering, MV refresh) to do work in the background. You don't see them, but they bill by the hour.

GUARDRAILS
The prepaid card

Resource monitors and budgets: a spending limit that texts you at 75% and freezes the card at 100%.

CHAPTER 01

The three cost buckets · credits vs dollars

01 · WHAT IS IT

A Snowflake bill has three buckets:

  • Compute, measured in credits: virtual warehouses (usually the biggest share), serverless features (Snowpipe, automatic clustering, serverless tasks…), and cloud services above a free allowance.
  • Storage, measured in average compressed TB per month: tables (including Time Travel and Fail-safe bytes), internal stages, and clone-owned data.
  • Data transfer, measured in bytes: data leaving the region or cloud (cross-region replication, unloading to another region, external function calls). Data coming in is generally not charged by Snowflake.
02 · WHY IT MATTERS

Snowflake is pay-per-use: no servers to buy, but also no natural ceiling. One warehouse left running all weekend, or one task scheduled every minute instead of every hour, can quietly double a bill. Senior engineers are expected to design for cost, explain it in business terms, and investigate when it spikes.

Credits are not dollars. A credit is a unit of compute. The price per credit depends on your edition (Standard, Enterprise, Business Critical, VPS), your cloud and region, and your contract (on-demand or pre-purchased capacity). That's why this module never quotes a fixed dollar price. Always reason in credits, then multiply by your rate.

03 · HOW IT WORKS · BUILD A SAMPLE BILL

INTERVIEW TRAP
"How much does an X-Small warehouse cost per hour?" Don't answer with a dollar number. Say: "1 credit per hour, billed per second with a 60-second minimum; the dollar value depends on edition, cloud, region and contract." That answer shows you understand the pricing model instead of quoting a blog post.
SENIOR ANGLE
Translate credits into the business's language: cost per dashboard refresh, per pipeline run, per team, per TB ingested. Unit cost is what finance and leadership actually track. "The orders pipeline costs 0.8 credits per run, 96 runs a day" is far more useful than "ETL_WH used 2,300 credits".
CHAPTER 02

Warehouse compute · size × running time

01 · WHAT IS IT

A virtual warehouse is a cluster of compute nodes. It burns credits only while it is running: from the moment it starts or resumes until it suspends. Each size up doubles the nodes and the credit rate:

SIZEXSSMLXL2XL3XL4XL
Credits / hour1248163264128

Standard (Gen1) warehouses. 5XL and 6XL exist too (256 and 512). Snowpark-optimized and Gen2 warehouses use different rates, so check the current credit table.

02 · WHY IT MATTERS

Warehouse compute is usually the largest line on the bill, and it's the part you control most directly. Three settings decide almost all of it: size, how long it stays running (auto-suspend), and how many clusters run at once.

The money leaks in a predictable place: idle time. A warehouse with no queries but not yet suspended bills at the full rate. Ten minutes of idle after every short query adds up fast.

03 · HOW IT WORKS · THE BILLING RULES
Per-second, 60 s minimum

Billing is per second, but each start or resume bills at least 60 seconds. A 5-second query on a suspended warehouse costs one full minute. Resizing up also bills the added compute with its own 60-second minimum.

Auto-suspend

After the last query finishes, a timer starts. When it hits AUTO_SUSPEND seconds with no new work, the warehouse suspends and billing stops. Warehouses created with SQL default to 600 s (10 minutes).

Auto-resume

With AUTO_RESUME = TRUE (the default), the next query wakes the warehouse automatically, usually within seconds. Users never notice the warehouse was asleep.

Multi-cluster

(Enterprise+) Extra clusters of the same size start when queries queue. Each running cluster bills like a separate warehouse. SCALING_POLICY = ECONOMY waits longer before adding clusters, so it saves credits but allows more queuing.

Formula: credits = credits_per_hour(size) × running_hours × running_clusters. Bigger is not automatically more expensive: if a big, parallel query runs twice as fast on double the size, the cost is the same and you get the answer sooner. But small queries can't use the extra nodes, so oversizing them simply doubles the rate for the same runtime.

INTERVIEW TRAP
"Set AUTO_SUSPEND to 1 second to save money." Not so fast. (1) Every resume bills at least 60 seconds, so a warehouse that suspends and resumes every few seconds can cost more. (2) Suspending drops the local SSD cache, so the next BI query re-reads data from remote storage and runs slower. Typical guidance: ~60 s for batch/ETL, a few minutes (often 5–10) for BI warehouses that benefit from a warm cache.
SENIOR ANGLE
Scale up vs scale out. Slow, heavy single queries (spilling, big joins) → a bigger size. Many concurrent users queuing → more clusters (multi-cluster), not a bigger size. And split workloads into separate warehouses (ETL, BI, ad-hoc, data science): you can size each correctly, isolate noisy neighbours, and see each team's cost clearly.

Right-sizing in practice

Signals it's too small

BYTES_SPILLED_TO_REMOTE_STORAGE > 0, long execution time on large scans, queries that would finish proportionally faster on a bigger size. Fix: size up (cost can stay flat if runtime halves).

Signals it's too big

Mostly small queries (seconds, little data scanned), low average load, no spilling. Doubling size gives no speed-up, just double the rate. Fix: size down and compare the runtime before/after.

Signals you need clusters

High QUEUED_OVERLOAD_TIME at peak hours while individual queries are fast. Fix: multi-cluster with sensible MIN/MAX_CLUSTER_COUNT, not a larger size.

CHAPTER 03

Storage cost · the bytes you can't see

01 · WHAT IS IT

Snowflake bills storage as the average number of compressed bytes stored per day across the month, priced per TB. It's not just your "live" data. The total includes:

  • Active bytes: the current micro-partitions of every table.
  • Time Travel bytes: old versions of changed or deleted micro-partitions, kept for DATA_RETENTION_TIME_IN_DAYS.
  • Fail-safe bytes: 7 more days after Time Travel ends, for permanent tables only. Not configurable; only Snowflake Support can recover from it.
  • Clone-owned bytes, internal stage files, and storage used by materialized views and search optimization.
02 · WHY IT MATTERS

Storage per TB is relatively cheap, so teams ignore it, until a high-churn table with 90-day Time Travel quietly holds 10× its own size in history. Or a forgotten stage keeps three years of raw CSVs. Or a dev clone of production drifts until it owns a full copy.

Storage problems grow silently: no query fails and no warehouse is busy. You only find them by looking at the storage views.

03 · HOW IT WORKS · WHY CHURN IS EXPENSIVE
Micro-partitions are immutable

Updating one row doesn't edit a file. Snowflake writes a new micro-partition with the change, and the old one moves into Time Travel. An UPDATE touching 1 row in each of 1,000 partitions rewrites 1,000 partitions.

Time Travel then Fail-safe

Replaced partitions stay in Time Travel for the retention period (default 1 day; up to 90 on Enterprise+ for permanent tables), then 7 days in Fail-safe, then they're purged.

Transient & temporary

Transient tables: Time Travel 0 or 1 day and no Fail-safe. Temporary: same, and they vanish at the end of the session. Ideal for staging and intermediate ETL tables you can rebuild.

Clones

A clone is zero-copy at creation: it points at the same partitions. Storage starts when either side changes. If you drop the source, partitions still referenced by the clone stay billed (RETAINED_FOR_CLONE_BYTES).

INTERVIEW TRAP
"We dropped the 50 TB table, why didn't storage drop?" A dropped permanent table is still in Time Travel (so UNDROP works), then Fail-safe for 7 days, and any partitions shared with a clone stay as long as the clone exists. Storage only falls after all three let go.
SENIOR ANGLE
Set retention per layer: raw/staging = transient with 0–1 day (it can be reloaded), curated/marts = permanent with a modest retention, and only truly critical tables get long Time Travel. Rebuild-every-night tables (CREATE OR REPLACE/truncate-reload) should almost always be transient, or each rebuild leaves a full copy in Fail-safe.
CHAPTER 04

Serverless & cloud services · compute you never sized

01 · WHAT IS IT

Serverless features run on compute that Snowflake provides and scales for you. There's no warehouse to size or suspend. You pay credits for the compute actually used, each feature at its own rate (Snowflake publishes a credit consumption table with per-feature multipliers).

Cloud services is Snowflake's "brain": login, metadata, query compilation and optimization, the result cache, access control. It also consumes credits, but most accounts pay nothing for it thanks to the 10% adjustment.

02 · WHY IT MATTERS

Serverless costs are the most common surprise on a bill. Nobody "turned anything on" this morning, yet automatic clustering on a churny table or a materialized view on a constantly-updated table can burn credits around the clock.

Also: resource monitors don't cover serverless features. They only watch warehouses. You need budgets and the serverless usage views to see this bucket.

03 · HOW IT WORKS · THE 10% CLOUD SERVICES ADJUSTMENT

Every day (in UTC), Snowflake compares your cloud services credits with 10% of that day's warehouse compute credits. You're only billed for the part above 10%. Try the scenarios:

INTERVIEW TRAP
"Dynamic tables are serverless, right?" No. Dynamic table refreshes run on the warehouse you specify, and that warehouse's normal billing applies. Same for user-managed tasks. Serverless tasks, Snowpipe, auto-clustering, MV maintenance, search optimization and QAS run on Snowflake-managed compute.
SENIOR ANGLE
Before enabling auto-clustering, search optimization or an MV, estimate the cost: SYSTEM$ESTIMATE_SEARCH_OPTIMIZATION_COSTS and SYSTEM$ESTIMATE_AUTOMATIC_CLUSTERING_COSTS exist for exactly this. Then compare maintenance credits against the warehouse credits saved by faster queries. If the table changes far more often than it's queried, the feature probably loses money.
CHAPTER 05

Guardrails · stop the spend before finance does

01 · WHAT IS IT

Four layers of protection:

  • Resource monitors: a credit quota per interval (daily, weekly, monthly, yearly or never-resetting) for the whole account or for specific warehouses, with triggers at percentages of that quota: NOTIFY, SUSPEND, SUSPEND_IMMEDIATE.
  • Budgets: a monthly spending limit for the account or for a custom group of objects, including serverless features, that sends notifications when spend is projected to exceed the limit.
  • STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_IN_SECONDS: cancels any single query that runs too long.
  • STATEMENT_QUEUED_TIMEOUT_IN_SECONDS: cancels a query that waits in the queue too long.
02 · WHY WE USE THEM

Usage-based pricing means a mistake becomes a bill: a cartesian join that runs for 40 hours on a 2XL, a scheduler bug that starts a job every minute, a warehouse resized to 4XL "just for one test". Guardrails turn an unbounded mistake into a bounded one, and tell a human early.

The default STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_IN_SECONDS is 172,800 seconds (2 days). On a large warehouse, a single bad query can burn thousands of credits before that default fires.

03 · HOW RESOURCE MONITORS WORK
Who creates them

By default only ACCOUNTADMIN can create resource monitors. It can grant MONITOR (view) and MODIFY (change) on a monitor to other roles.

Account vs warehouse

Account-level (one per account): counts all warehouses and can suspend them all. Warehouse-level: assigned to one or more warehouses; each warehouse can be assigned to only one monitor.

The three actions

NOTIFY: alert only. SUSPEND: suspend, but let running queries finish (some overshoot). SUSPEND_IMMEDIATE: suspend and cancel running queries. Thresholds can be above 100%.

What they don't see

Only warehouse credits. Serverless features (Snowpipe, auto-clustering, serverless tasks…) and AI services are not tracked or stopped. Use budgets for those.

Once a monitor suspends a warehouse, it can't be resumed until the next interval starts, the quota or threshold is raised, or the warehouse is removed from the monitor. Monitors check usage periodically, not per second, so expect a little overshoot even with SUSPEND_IMMEDIATE.

Timeouts: the per-query guardrail

STATEMENT_TIMEOUT_IN_SECONDS

Maximum run time for a statement, after which Snowflake cancels it. Default 172,800 s (2 days). Set it on the account, user, session or warehouse. If both the warehouse and the session set a value, the smaller non-zero one wins. Typical: 1–2 hours on ETL warehouses, 10–30 minutes on ad-hoc/BI.

STATEMENT_QUEUED_TIMEOUT_IN_SECONDS

Maximum time a statement can wait in a warehouse's queue before being cancelled. Default 0 = no limit. Useful on BI warehouses so dashboards fail fast with a clear error instead of hanging during an overload, and to stop queued jobs piling up behind a stuck one.

INTERVIEW TRAP
"We have an account-level resource monitor at 100% SUSPEND, so cost is fully capped." Not true: serverless features and AI services keep running, SUSPEND lets in-flight queries finish, and checks aren't instant. Also, suspending every warehouse mid-month can stop production pipelines. That's an outage, so many teams only NOTIFY at account level and SUSPEND at warehouse level for non-critical warehouses.
SENIOR ANGLE
Layer it: account monitor with NOTIFY at 75/90/100% (early warning for the whole bill) → warehouse monitors with SUSPEND for dev/ad-hoc and SUSPEND_IMMEDIATE at ~110% → budgets to cover serverless and per-team groups → statement timeouts on every warehouse. Phrase budgets carefully in interviews: they notify (and in newer releases can trigger actions); they don't suspend warehouses by themselves.
CHAPTER 06

Visibility & attribution · who spent what

01 · WHAT IS IT

Snowflake exposes usage through three families of views:

  • SNOWFLAKE.ACCOUNT_USAGE: account-wide views with about 1 year of history, including dropped objects, but with latency (typically from ~45 minutes up to a few hours depending on the view).
  • INFORMATION_SCHEMA (in every database): views and table functions with near-zero latency but short retention (often 7 days up to 6 months depending on the function) and no dropped objects.
  • SNOWFLAKE.ORGANIZATION_USAGE: usage across all accounts in the organization, including views that show spend in currency. Typically accessed from the organization account/ORGADMIN context and with longer latency.
02 · WHY IT MATTERS

You can't optimize what you can't see, and you can't make teams responsible for cost unless you can show each team its number. Attribution answers: which warehouse, which user, which pipeline, which query pattern, which business unit.

Access: by default only ACCOUNTADMIN can query the SNOWFLAKE database. Grant IMPORTED PRIVILEGES or a SNOWFLAKE database role (such as USAGE_VIEWER) to a FinOps/monitoring role.

03 · HOW ATTRIBUTION WORKS
Warehouse level

WAREHOUSE_METERING_HISTORY: credits per warehouse per hour (compute and cloud services columns). Easiest and most reliable: one warehouse per team = clean chargeback.

Query level

QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY: compute credits attributed to individual queries on shared warehouses. Per Snowflake's docs it excludes idle time and very short queries, and arrives with several hours of latency. Check the docs for current rules.

Tags

Query tags (ALTER SESSION SET QUERY_TAG = …) label queries by pipeline or app. Object tags (CREATE TAG cost_center) label warehouses, databases, users; join TAG_REFERENCES to metering views for chargeback.

INTERVIEW TRAP
"Sum the credits in QUERY_ATTRIBUTION_HISTORY and you get the warehouse bill." No: idle time (and cloud services) isn't attributed to any query, so the sum is lower than WAREHOUSE_METERING_HISTORY. The gap is your idle cost, which is a useful metric on its own.

Make tagging enforced, not optional: set a default QUERY_TAG per service user (ALTER USER svc_dbt SET QUERY_TAG = …), have dbt/Airflow inject JSON tags ({"team":"finance","pipeline":"orders_daily"}), tag every warehouse with cost_center, and publish a weekly cost-by-team dashboard. Visibility alone changes behaviour.
CHAPTER 07

Investigation playbook · "the bill went up 40%"

01 · WHAT IS IT

A repeatable, top-down method for explaining a cost increase: bucket → service → warehouse → idle vs busy → workload → individual query or object. At each level you run one query, read the result, and either find a root cause or drill one level deeper.

02 · WHY IT MATTERS

It's the single most common senior cost question in interviews, and in real life. Interviewers aren't looking for one magic answer. They want to see a structured approach, the right system views, the usual root causes, and fixes plus prevention so it doesn't happen again.

03 · HOW IT WORKS · STEP THROUGH A REAL INVESTIGATION

Last month: 10,000 credits. This month: 14,000. Press Next. Each step shows the query you'd run, an illustrative result, and what it tells you. Root causes light up on the board as you find them.

INTERVIEW TRAP
Jumping straight to "make the warehouses smaller". First locate the increase (which bucket, which warehouse, idle or busy, which workload); otherwise you might shrink a warehouse that wasn't the problem and slow down every dashboard. Also check whether the increase is legitimate growth (more data, more users, a new product launch) before calling it waste.
SENIOR ANGLE
Close every investigation with prevention: a resource monitor on the warehouse that leaked, statement timeouts, a weekly "top 20 queries by credits" report, a change process for warehouse size and auto-suspend (ideally managed in Terraform/code review), and an alert when daily credits exceed the 30-day average by X%.
